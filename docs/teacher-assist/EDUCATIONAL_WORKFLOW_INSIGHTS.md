# TeacherAssist Educational Workflow Insights

## Real Teacher Pain Points

- Teachers spend significant unpaid time after work planning, grading, recording grades, and preparing materials.
- ELA grading is especially time-consuming because it is subjective, writing-heavy, rubric-based, and feedback-intensive.
- A single ELA assignment can take hours to grade and record after school.
- Teachers often work late into the evening because grading and planning do not fit into the school day.
- Reducing after-hours workload is a primary value proposition.

## Planning Insights

- Teachers are excited by hosted pacing guides because pacing guides organize real instructional work.
- Teachers may want to plan four or five weeks at once when teaching a module.
- Teachers think in instructional arcs/modules, not only isolated weeks.
- Planning should support module duration rather than forcing everything into weekly planning.
- A module may span four weeks, five weeks, or another teacher-defined duration.
- Teachers need flexibility to plan by pacing guide, module, unit, grading period, or week.
- Teachers also need visible review cues so generated plans can be checked quickly for standards alignment, pacing realism, differentiation, and materials fit before classroom use.

## Grading Insights

- Teachers need help with grading, feedback, and grade recording.
- AI should help shift teachers from grading-from-scratch mode into review-and-confirm mode.
- Repetitive feedback drafting is a major opportunity.
- The system should support rubric-aligned review and teacher confirmation.
- Assignment workflows need a reliable teacher-owned foundation first so later QR, upload, grading, and mastery features inherit class/subject/standards context instead of rebuilding it ad hoc.

## Printable Assignment / QR Insights

- Teachers should be able to generate blank printable assignment templates.
- Templates may include blank writing pages, lined pages, short-answer pages, rubric-attached pages, or reading-response pages.
- Printouts should be generated per student using anonymous STUDENT #.
- If a teacher chooses two pages per student, each student should receive two pages with that student’s QR assignment-page identity.
- The first printable foundation should work without OCR or AI and should expose QR-safe packet metadata before later scan/upload phases exist.
- When pages are scanned/uploaded later, TeacherAssist should group pages by assignment, anonymous student number, class, subject, grading period, and page order.

## Collaborative Planning Insights

- Multiple teachers in the same grade level often teach the same curriculum and pacing guide.
- Teachers may want shared/common instructional plans with personal customization.
- Teachers still need autonomy to adjust plans to fit:
  - classroom behavior
  - pacing differences
  - intervention needs
  - instructional style
  - small-group strategy
- Teachers do not want to repeatedly regenerate the same curriculum every school year.

## Curriculum Reuse Insights

- Teachers often reuse instructional materials year over year.
- Previous school-year artifacts should remain accessible and reusable.
- Teachers should be able to carry forward:
  - plans
  - quizzes
  - rubrics
  - templates
  - resources
  - pacing structures
  - instructional notes
- Teachers may want to regenerate only sections impacted by curriculum changes.
- Teachers benefit when targeted rewrites preserve the rest of a reviewed plan instead of forcing a full-plan replacement.
- Reusability across school years is a major long-term value driver.

## Teacher Personalization Insights

- Teachers may start from a shared plan but modify activities, pacing, vocabulary emphasis, differentiation, assessments, or instructional sequencing.
- Some personalization requires only manual edits.
- Some personalization may benefit from AI-assisted adaptation/regeneration.
- Teachers should control when AI is invoked.
